## Comment 1 — Table 2 t-tests

> *"Could you add t-tests for significance to Table 2, comparing each population to the 'all respondents' population? Right now, there's a lot of data in this table, and it's not possible to tell which differences are significant."*

**Response.** We thank the reviewer for this useful suggestion. We have added significance stars to every gender-group cell in Table 2. Each star reflects a two-sample test of proportions comparing the gender group's rate of the field to the rate among the remaining respondents (`*` p<0.10, `**` p<0.05, `***` p<0.01). The expanded Note beneath Table 2 documents the test and flags the small-N gender groups (transgender women n=20, transgender men n=59, gender diverse/questioning n=71) where the asymptotic approximation is weakest.

## Comment 2 — Table 3 standardization + t-tests vs. cisgender men

> *"In Table 3, for the 'General High School Experience Index,' I would recommend converting your index into a standardized scale, with 0 as the average, negative 1 represents 1 standard deviation below the mean, 2 represents 2 standard deviations above the mean, etc. … it would be useful to test for significant differences here (perhaps against a reference category — cis men have the highest scores so I might use them as the references category for t-tests)."*

**Response.** We have implemented both of the reviewer's recommendations. Table 3 now reports the high-school experience index in standardized form (mean 0 and standard deviation 1 over the analytical sample of n = 7,483). We adopt cisgender men as the omitted reference category, and each non-reference row of Table 3 reports the standardized mean along with the difference relative to cisgender men in standard-deviation units, with t-test significance indicated by stars (`*` p<0.10, `**` p<0.05, `***` p<0.01). The largest gap is for gender diverse/questioning students at approximately 0.73 standard deviations below cisgender men (p<0.001); all other non-reference categories also differ significantly from cisgender men at p<0.001, with the exception of transgender women (p=0.073), where the small subsample (n=20) limits precision. The corresponding prose in §Findings → High School Experiences now reports magnitudes in standard-deviation units.

## Comment 3 — Figure 5 equations and standardized index throughout

> *"Could you please write out the equation(s) used to produce the coefficients in Figure 5 (and the other coefplots)? It's not currently very clear — is this all one model? What controls are included? Again, I would encourage you to use a standardized index score here as well. … I would like to see these standardized results reported throughout most of your tables and figures rather than the various indexes."*

**Response.** We have made three changes in response. First, we added a new paragraph in §Data Analysis (Methods) that writes out the two OLS specifications underlying Figures 5–8 — one unconditional (Specification 1) and one with demographic controls (Specification 2). For the high-school-experience outcome (Figure 5), Specification 2 includes race/ethnicity and parental-education indicators. For the three worry-index outcomes (Figures 6–8), Specification 2 additionally controls for the standardized high-school-experience index. In all specifications, cisgender man is the omitted reference category, so each gender-group coefficient is interpretable as the difference in the standardized outcome relative to cisgender men. Second, we standardized all four outcomes (the high-school-experience index and the three worry-index PCA constructs) to mean 0 and standard deviation 1 over the unconditional analytical sample, so that all coefficients in Figures 5–8 are now reported in standard-deviation units; this is also reflected in updated Table 4, Figure 5–8 notes, and Footnote 9 describing the index construction. Third, while verifying the new standardized magnitudes against our regression output, we caught and corrected a directional error in the previously submitted Figure 6 discussion paragraph. The earlier text stated that "general worries of transgender men, transgender women, and nonbinary or gender questioning students was significantly lower than cisgender men"; in fact, our regression shows the opposite — these groups have **higher** general worries than cisgender men, consistent with the paragraph immediately preceding it. We have corrected the language and are grateful to the reviewer for the prompt to revisit these specifications, which surfaced the inconsistency.
